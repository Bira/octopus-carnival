This is what everyone's been waiting for, right? In the assumed setting of
DFRPG, "low-level" magic items are common enough that they can often be found in
"magic shops" in cities or larger towns. The selection presented here represents
those. There are many more, and more powerful items, but those are not for sale
and are usually found in dungeons.

# Potions

Potions follow the same basic rules as the non-magical concoctions from the
previous post, but they're magic items. This means they can be detected as such
by someone with Magery, and their ongoing effects can be ended by Dispel
Magic against the skill of the maker. That's 15 for potions bought in town, but
it could be higher or lower for found potions.

Multiple doses of the same potion usually aren't cumulative, but different
potions can affect someone at the same time.

This is where you have grenades that are more damaging versions of the
alchemical ones, as well as one that puts people to sleep and another one that
prevents wizardly casters from using magic for a while. Drinkable potions
include a whole range of healing potions, attribute boosters for ST, DX, Basic
Speed and Perception, stuff that gives you flight, invisibility or extra DR for
a while, and Thieves' Oil which makes you extra-slippery.

# Scrolls

Each scroll contains a single use spell. There's some rules about how long it
takes to draw and unroll them in combat, and after that you take twice as long
as normal to cast the spell.

To create a scroll, you decide what spell is inside (pick from any list!) and
then whether the scroll is wizardly, clerical, druidic or universal. Universal
scrolls can be used by anyone, even a "mundane". The others can only be used by
characters with the appropriate power trait.

Finally, a scroll can be charged or not. A charged scroll contains all the
energy necessary to power the spell, and costs nothing from the reader. An
uncharged one drains the reader's FP. In any case the scroll's monetary cost
scales with its energy cost.

Delvers prefer universal charged scrolls, of course, but as you might imagine
those are the most expensive of all. Further restrictions decrease the
cost. Scrolls bought in town have a skill level of 15 both for casting the spell
and for resisting dispelling attempts. Found scrolls will often have higher
skill.

# Magic Weapons and Armor

The stars of the show! Weapons and armor most often contain permanent, always-on
enchantments, and this is the list of enchantments purchasable by PCs. To create
a magic item, you first buy the base "mundane" weapon or armor with all its
modifiers, and then apply the enchantments you want to it. These add directly to
the final price.

When a PC buys a magic item in town, they get to decide whether the enchantment
is wizardly, clerical, or druidic (which will matter for rules described in DF:
Exploits). These purchases can happen either in play or as part of character
creation, though few delvers will have the money to spare at that time.

Visits to Ye Olde Magic Shoppe are abstracted by default, just like all other
shopping in the game, and the GM decides what's available every time the PCs
return to Town from adventuring.

The most noticeable class of enchantments here are your standard "plusses":
to-hit, damage and Parry for weapons, Defense Bonus and Block for shields,
Defense Bonus and DR for armor. One or two levels are available for each,
depending on the enchantment.

Weapons can also benefit from one of a set of elemental damage adders (flaming,
lightning, freezing), from a Penetrating enchantment that helps get past DR, and
from being made magically unbreakable. Armor and shields can have their weight
reduced, and anything can be made glowy so that it illuminates like a torch on
command.

That's it for purchasable magic. Want something more powerful? Go delve a
dungeon and find something from the expanded GM-only list from Exploits.
