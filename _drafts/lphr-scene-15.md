# Intro

If we were simply reading the book, we would already be done with
adventure 01. But we're playing, and there is a vital part of the adventure's
capstone dungeon delve that I've been putting off until now: the looting!

I've ran this delve as a "brutal raid", so our PCs were mainly interested in
clearing the cultists infesting the place as fast as possible, and whenever they
found some loot they just made a mental note to get to it later.

Well, Nox has been defeated, and it's "Later" now. The book doesn't really
mention how often the diabolists here checked back with Thrune, but I figure our
heroes have enough time to gather and tally all the loot, though I guess they
might need to call in a couple of Silver Raven teams to help haul it all back to
base.

# [GM] Delver: The Lootening

Unfortunately, the very fact that Thrune's minions know where the Monastery is
and have successfully raided it in the past means that they cannot use it as a
base, so they have to stay in the somewhat cramped Wasp Nest for now. But they
can still loot the place!

I think we can abstract our actual procedure here. The PCs use a Silver Raven
figurine to call their Freedom Fighters and Sneaks teams and strip this place
bare. I'll roll Perception for the PCs when stuff is hidden, but we can assume
the teams take care of carting the loot out, and dropping Azvernathy somewhere
beyond city walls with instructions to never show his face in this town again.

The next section gives talks about the XP they gained from this delve. Then we
get a high-level overview of what the PCs found, and what they did it with it
after bringing it to the Wasp Nest.

And finally, for the completionists, you get a full tally of the loot found in
the dungeon, complete with costs and weights in the format I use for my
home-brewed gear tallying script.

# [GM] XP Rewards

I'm not sure I specified how I'm handling XP here. That's in part because I
hadn't 100% settled on a system yet. I went with the one suggested in Exploits
for a while but I think I'm going to go with something considerably more
generous given the slow pace of play in this campaign and the lack of proper
"sessions".

Every time the PCs complete a significant quest, they get 5 character points. If
we have a capital-P part in a Hell's Rebels book that has one single linear
goal, that's a significant quest. If we have something that's labeled as a
Mission, usually in the middle part of a book, that's also a significant
quest. So, for example, the Aria Park Riot was one such quest, then we had thew
various missions I played, and then the capstone delve was another quest.

Additionally, every time the book specifically calls out a story-based XP award
for something, they gain 1 character point if they achieve that goal.

And finally, they get another 5 character points at the end of the adventure.

We're using this new system starting from the capstone delve in adventure 1. So
at the end of this delve, they got:

- 1 point for gaining access to level 2 of the dungeon.
- 1 point for deactivating the Cubic Gate.
- 1 point for recovering the soul tomes.
- 1 point for discovering the clue within (see below).
- 5 points for clearing the dungeon.
- 5 points for finishing the adventure.

That's 14 points each, making all of them into 289-point characters (they were
at 275 when they started the delve).

We'll deal with character advancement in a later post.

# [Player] Loot Disposition

Obviously we're taking the money right away and taking it into the common party
stash. I want to use this to upgrade the PC's gear, and most of what's left when
I'm done will go into the Raven treasury. Already that's $8700 right there.

Next we have our "art objects", mundane but valuable items with no associated
game benefits. We're keeping the Victocora signet rings to hand off to Rexus,
but everything else should be fenced. The rest consists mainly of books, with
some miscellaneous fancy writing and reading tools thrown in. Urist will only
part with the books if he's sure they can go to someone who will make good use
of them _outside_ of Cheliax, but Jania assures him she can make it happen (and
as the GM I confirm it). She's going to talk to Lady Mialari Docur in the space
between adventures, and I she will handle the rest.

Our loot pile in this category totals a whopping $51250 and weights 43kg. The
process of carting it around and selling it probably takes a couple of weeks,
but we can sum up the results here. Per the standard rules, that amount up there
is the loot's nominal worth. Our heroes will be able to fence it for 40% of that
value, or 50% if one of them can win a contest of Merchant vs. 15. Jania does
win, so we're looking at $25625 for the lot. 84 gold, 1 silver, 5 copper.

Next we have our "tools", items which have a game effect but are not what I'd
consider "equipment". This includes consumables like potions and scrolls, as
well as the useful tomes we found and the Bookplate of Recall. I think we're
keeping everything here except for the Bookplate. I roll another Merchant roll
for it, and it gets sold off for $5000 (12 gold, 10 silver).

And finally we have our "Equipment". Armor and weapons and things that can be
worn and wielded for continuous effect. Here we have Nox's and Azvernathy's
armor and weapons, the Headband of Will +1, the Ring of Swimming, a scroll belt,
and a humble belt pouch. Total value $61134, total weight around 39kg.

Our heroes are keeping everything but Nox's glaive, which no one specializes in,
and Raul's shield which is redundant. That's $240 (12 silvers) added to our
party treasury. Art will take Nox's Armor, Urist will take Raul's mace and
armor. Jade takes the Headband and Ring of Swimming, Jania the scroll belt.

I'll charge the group a $500 "refit" fee for the two suits of armor and the
mace, which is roughly 10% of their value. They have to be cleansed of all that
Asmodean iconography and Raul's armor has to be adjusted to fit Urist's dwarven
frame. Total fee: $1500 (3 gold, 15 silver).

## Room F7: Meditation Garden

The books Nox was perusing here are soul tomes created by Yiliv the
Scrivenite. They became mundane books when the original owners of their memories
died, but remain valuable for the knowledge they contain.

In addition to that knowlegde, they also contain stories about how the archive
used to contain a copy of a section of the _Broken-Winged Crane_, a terrible
eldritch tome about demons of all kinds. They also make mention of a scholar
named "Trailbrazer Huni" who visited the place and convinced the archivists to
let him study the book for a single hour. The PCs have not read this one yet,
but when they do they should have no problem decoding the anagram and figuring
out this was Thrune in disguise.
